Time to put Module 1 together. No code: just paper and a pencil (or any drawing app).

## The task

Pick an app you use every day: a banking app, a food delivery app, a messaging app. Draw:

1. **The client:** a box for the app on your phone.
2. **The server:** a box for the company's server.
3. **The API:** arrows between them. Label three arrows with a request the app probably makes, such as `GET /orders` or `POST /messages`. Write the method and a path you invent.
4. **The database:** a cylinder next to the server. Inside it, list three tables the app probably has, each with three columns.
5. **One journey:** pick one thing you do in the app (send a message, pay for a coffee) and number the steps from your tap to the result on screen, like the worked examples in this module.

<Exercise title="Check your drawing">
Go through your drawing and answer:

- Is anything that involves money, permissions or shared data happening only on the client?
- Does every arrow have a method and a path?
- Does every table have an id column?

<Fragment slot="hint">Compare with the worked example in lesson 1.3: the balance came from the server, not the phone.</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="solution">
Here is one for a food delivery app:

- **Requests:** `GET /restaurants`, `POST /orders`, `GET /orders/31`.
- **Tables:**
  - **restaurants** (id, name, address)
  - **orders** (id, customer_id, restaurant_id, status)
  - **customers** (id, name, email)
- **Journey:**
  1. Tap **Order**.
  2. The app sends `POST /orders` with the basket.
  3. The server checks that the restaurant is open and the payment works.
  4. The server saves a row in `orders` inside a transaction.
  5. The server answers `201 Created`.
  6. The app shows "Order placed".

Yours will differ, and that is fine. What matters is that the decisions happen on the server.
</Fragment>
