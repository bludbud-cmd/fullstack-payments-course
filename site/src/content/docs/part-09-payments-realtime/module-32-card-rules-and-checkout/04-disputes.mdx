**Goal:** By the end of this lesson you can explain what a dispute and a chargeback are, who takes part and what each one has to prove, and you can walk a PayDeck dispute from the customer's message to the refund, using only tools you have already built.

## Why it matters

Card payments come with a promise to the cardholder: if something goes wrong that is not your fault, you can get your money back. That promise is a big part of why people trust cards, and it is an issuer's job to keep it. It is also where fraud, mistakes and angry customers meet, so it needs a calm, written process, not improvisation.

## The idea

### Refund or dispute?

A **refund** (lesson 32.3) is the easy way back: the shop agrees and returns the money. A **dispute** is what happens when the shop will not or cannot: the cardholder goes to their **issuer** instead and says the payment was wrong. If the issuer agrees, it takes the money back from the shop's side through the card network. That forced return is called a **chargeback**.

### Why people dispute

Card networks sort disputes into groups. Visa, for example, uses four:

- **Fraud**: "I didn't make this payment." The card was stolen or its details were copied.
- **Authorization**: the payment should never have been approved, for example the card was already reported lost.
- **Processing errors**: charged twice, charged the wrong amount, charged in the wrong currency.
- **Consumer disputes**: "I paid and never got it", "it was not as described", "I cancelled and they kept charging me".

Each group has its own rules about what evidence counts.

### How a chargeback travels

1. The cardholder tells the issuer what went wrong.
2. The issuer checks the claim. For clear fraud it usually freezes the card straight away, and often gives the cardholder a **provisional credit**: the money back for now, while the case is open.
3. The issuer files the chargeback through the card network to the shop's acquirer, which takes the money from the shop and tells it.
4. The shop can accept, or **fight it** by sending evidence: a delivery receipt, a signature, proof the customer used the service. This is called **representment**.
5. If the two sides still disagree, the network decides (**arbitration**), and the losing side usually pays a fee.

Deadlines run through all of it. A cardholder usually has a limited time to dispute, often around 120 days after the payment, depending on the network and the reason, and the shop has a limited time to answer. A missed deadline loses the case.

### Where PayDeck fits

PayDeck is the issuer, and in this course there is no real network or shop to send a chargeback to. What PayDeck can do, and what real issuers must do well, is the **issuer's side**: listen, look at the facts, protect the card, decide, put the money back, and keep a record. You already have every tool for that:

| Step | PayDeck tool | From |
|---|---|---|
| Find the payment | `GET /api/cards/{id}/payments`, as support | 32.2 |
| Stop more damage | Freeze the card, as support | 30.2 |
| Give the money back | `POST /api/admin/card-payments/{id}/refund` | 32.3 |
| Prove what was done, and by whom | The audit log | 30.5 |

What PayDeck does not have yet is a **case**: a record of the dispute itself, its reason, its deadline and its outcome. The exercise below asks you to design one.

### Fraud versus "friendly fraud"

Some disputes are honest mistakes by the cardholder: they did not recognise the shop's name on their statement, or a family member used the card. Some are dishonest: the person got the goods and claims they did not. The industry calls that **friendly fraud**, and it is why shops keep evidence and why issuers look at a card's history before deciding. PayDeck's saved declines and payments (lesson 32.2) are exactly that history.

## Worked example: a dispute in PayDeck

Asha writes to support: "I see a payment of $12.50 at Corner Café, but I was at home all day." Sam, from support, works the case.

**1. Find the payment.** Sam opens Asha's Groceries card in the staff screens (lesson 30.4), or asks the API:

```sh
curl -s localhost:8080/api/cards/$G/payments -H "Authorization: Bearer $SAM" \
  | jq -c '.[] | {id, merchant, amountCents, status, createdAt}'
```

He finds the café payment, `512a802c-…`, approved.

**2. Protect the card.** If Asha did not make it, someone else has the card's details. Sam freezes the card first, before anything else, so nothing more can be spent: support may freeze but not unfreeze (lesson 30.2), so only Asha can turn it back on, once she has a new card.

```sh
curl -s -X POST localhost:8080/api/cards/$G/freeze -H "Authorization: Bearer $SAM"
```

From now on any payment attempt on that card is declined with `CARD_FROZEN`, and saved, which also shows whether the thief keeps trying.

**3. Decide and give the money back.** A clear fraud claim from a customer with a clean history is refunded. Sam refunds the payment exactly as in lesson 32.3, and Asha's balance is back to where it was.

**4. The record.** The audit log now says that Sam froze the card and refunded the payment, and when. If anyone later asks "why did PayDeck give Asha $12.50?", the answer is in the log, not in someone's memory.

<Aside type="note" title="Real issuers go one step further">
After step 3, a real issuer would file a chargeback so that the shop's side pays for the fraud, not the issuer. PayDeck's refund comes out of the "outside world" account, which here stands for that whole process.
</Aside>

<Exercise title="Design a dispute case">
Sketch a `disputes` table for PayDeck: the columns, the possible statuses and how a dispute moves between them, and which PayDeck rules should apply (who may open one, who may decide). Write it as a `CREATE TABLE` statement and a list of status changes. You do not need to build it.

<Fragment slot="hint">Think about what a support agent needs to see when they pick up someone else's case on a Monday: what was disputed, why, by whom, by when it must be decided, and what happened.</Fragment>
<Fragment slot="solution">

```sql
CREATE TABLE disputes (
    id               UUID PRIMARY KEY,
    card_payment_id  UUID         NOT NULL REFERENCES card_payments (id),
    opened_by        UUID         NOT NULL REFERENCES users (id),
    reason           VARCHAR(20)  NOT NULL CHECK (reason IN ('FRAUD', 'NOT_RECEIVED', 'DUPLICATE', 'WRONG_AMOUNT', 'OTHER')),
    description      VARCHAR(1000) NOT NULL,
    status           VARCHAR(20)  NOT NULL CHECK (status IN ('OPEN', 'WON', 'LOST', 'WITHDRAWN')),
    respond_by       TIMESTAMPTZ  NOT NULL,
    decided_by       UUID         REFERENCES users (id),
    created_at       TIMESTAMPTZ  NOT NULL,
    decided_at       TIMESTAMPTZ
);
```

Status changes:

- `OPEN` → `WON`: staff decide for the cardholder; the payment is refunded in the same transaction.
- `OPEN` → `LOST`: staff decide for the shop; no money moves, and the cardholder is told why.
- `OPEN` → `WITHDRAWN`: the cardholder recognises the payment after all.
- Nothing ever leaves `WON`, `LOST` or `WITHDRAWN`.

Rules: the owner or cardholder may open a dispute on an `APPROVED` payment of their card (a new `CardAction`), and only one open dispute per payment (a partial unique index). Only `SUPPORT` and `ADMIN` may decide, with `@PreAuthorize`, and every change is audited. `respond_by` lets a staff screen sort cases by deadline.
</Fragment>

## Common mistakes

<Aside type="caution" title="Refunding before protecting the card">
If the card details were stolen, every minute it stays active is another chance to use them. Freeze first, then investigate.
</Aside>

<Aside type="caution" title="Deciding from memory">
"I think we refunded that last week" is how money gets paid back twice. Look at the payment's status and the audit log; that is what they are for.
</Aside>

<Aside type="caution" title="Treating every dispute as fraud">
Many disputes are a shop name the person did not recognise. Showing a clear merchant name and the card's nickname in the app (as PayDeck does) prevents a surprising number of them.
</Aside>

## Recap

- A **dispute** is a cardholder asking their issuer to reverse a payment; a **chargeback** is the issuer taking the money back from the shop's side through the network.
- Disputes fall into groups such as fraud, authorization, processing errors and consumer disputes, each with its own evidence and **deadlines**.
- The shop can answer with evidence (**representment**); the network decides if they still disagree.
- In PayDeck the issuer's side is: find the payment, **freeze** the card, **refund**, and let the **audit log** record it.
